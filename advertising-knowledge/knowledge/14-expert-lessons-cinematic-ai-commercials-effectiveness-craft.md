A2. Interview with Dave Clark (Promise), 30-second spot that won second place and $500,000 in an xAI contest - Curious Refuge - https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0C97pAlyb8I - date UNKNOWN (INFERENCE Feb 2026, after the Super Bowl) - about 13:19 - read in full: yes. VENDOR.

A3. "How commercials are made" (exact title not captured) - Mark Bone - https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3KV2MPSrlEs - date UNKNOWN (INFERENCE about late 2021) - about 10:19 - read in full: yes. COMMUNITY.
1. Chain: client, agency pitches campaign ideas, client approves one direction, agency briefs production companies, they present directors.
2. Agency narrows reels to "their top three choices" and briefs those three directors.
3. Director gets "hopefully a couple days", sometimes "tomorrow", for the treatment; his example was "a couple pages": how he will direct it, the look, the vibe, the brand.
4. After winning: full script, agency sign-off, then pre-production.
5. Location brief to a scout; pick top five from photos, visit, choose one.
6. Casting brief (age, look, skills); one job needed about 150 in-person castings over 2 days.
7. Director's and agency's top choices for location, cast and wardrobe go to the client.
8. Tech scout with cinematographer, art director, gaffer and grip; the assistant director schedules.
9. Wardrobe and hair decided before the shoot: "once you get to set, you can't really be making these decisions".
10. PPM: a document of about 180 pages, 30 to 45 people on the call.
11. Camera prep: tests, lens choice, wardrobe on camera, light position at the location.
12. His mantra: "it's better to be stressed than it is to be confused". Clients often react only on set (a lens brand banned lens flares).

A4. "Easiest way to make winning ads on Meta" (exact title not captured) - Dara Denney - https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=p4yXXdnCgWg - date UNKNOWN - about 15:23 - read in full: yes. COMMUNITY (mentions Motion's agent).
1. Three basics: the big idea (Ogilvy), pattern recognition, prioritisation.
2. Practise stating each ad's big idea in one line.
3. Prioritise by what is easiest and quickest to make and what has most evidence; validate a big idea with a static, an internal creator or founder content before a costly shoot.
4. AI animation ads take a lot of time and tend to give "a quick hit", not an evergreen asset like founder or partnership ads.
5. Framework 1: your next winner is probably already in the account (above $100k a month or $1M total spend): take the top 20 creatives of the last year, extract big ideas, move them to another format.
6. Her example: the winning idea was "5 minutes", not "get ready with me"; the ad spent "well over 2 million dollars".
7. A big idea should appear in several formats and from several creators; she says a person sees ads "seven times" before buying.
8. Framework 2 for accounts with little data: search TikTok and Reels for the idea; strongest signal is small accounts going viral with a format; note repeated phrases, visuals, settings.
9. Iterate on an ad "until it stops working".

A6. "5 Techniques For Framing Cinematic Shots" - In Depth Cine - script page https://www.indepthcine.com/videos/cinematic-framing (video GtPqDPhx-EI) - 30 Jul 2023 - length UNKNOWN - read in full: yes (creator's own script page). COMMUNITY.
1. Every frame = subject plus background; choose the background on purpose, it carries information.
2. Depth reads as more cinematic: move the subject away from walls.
3. His Super 35 groups: wide about 16-25mm, medium about 35-65mm, telephoto about 75mm and longer.
4. Wide lens close to the subject shows more background and distorts; long lens from further away compresses, with shallow depth of field.
5. Rule of thirds; horizon on the top or bottom third.
6. Leading lines; frame within a frame (door, window, mirror).
7. Tighter framing feels more intimate; wider feels neutral and observational.
8. Eye level = empathy and normality; low angle = power; high angle = vulnerability.
9. Balance through positive and negative space, symmetrical or asymmetrical.
10. Breathing room in front of the subject; cutting headroom makes a shot tighter and tenser.

A7. "How Cinematographers Use Hard Vs. Soft Light" - In Depth Cine - https://www.indepthcine.com/videos/hard-vs-soft-light (video yf1vxrrTO_g) - 28 May 2023 - length UNKNOWN - read in full: yes. COMMUNITY.
1. Hard light: sharp shadow edge from a direct source. Soft light: gradual edge from a diffused or bounced source.
2. Soft light flatters skin; "historically been used for beauty commercials".
3. Soft light also reads as natural, calm, romantic or comedic.
4. Hard light: drama, contrast, tension, texture; a hard backlight separates the subject from the background.
5. Two ways to soften: diffusion and bounce.
6. Lee 216 White Diffusion costs "a stop and a half" of light; more softness means less light.
7. Diffusion frames 8x8, 12x12, 20x20; fabrics silk, grid cloth, muslin.
8. Bounce: further from the subject is softer and weaker; the bounce colour tints the light.
9. Book light = bounce, then diffuse: very soft, needs high output.
10. Hard light: direct source, fewer sources, a mirror, or a bare practical bulb.

A8. "Editing Transitions Every Filmmaker Should Know" - In Depth Cine - https://www.indepthcine.com/videos/editing-transitions (video SxfRbTOaCuY) - 21 May 2023 - length UNKNOWN - read in full: yes. COMMUNITY.
1. Cut speed changes the feel (hurried or calm).
2. Tight to wide gives context; wide to tight focuses attention.
3. Insert = brief close-up of a detail: "this thing is important".
4. Jump cut breaks continuity: tension, or passing time in vlog style.
5. Cross cutting between locations builds suspense and energy.
6. Graphic match cut links shape, colour or movement; it must be planned at the shoot (overlay a freeze frame, or trace on the monitor).
7. Contrast cut: loud to quiet, or opposite colour or tone.
8. J-cut: next shot's sound arrives first. L-cut: previous sound continues over the new shot.
9. Dissolve = time passing or dreamlike; fade to black = closure.
10. Hidden cut: cut on fast movement that continues the previous movement.

A9. "Sonic Branding" (audio episode, official transcript) - Twenty Thousand Hertz, Dallas Taylor - https://www.20k.org/episodes/sonicbranding - 20 Aug 2018 - length UNKNOWN - read in full: yes. COMMUNITY (guests are vendors).
1. Audio logo = short distinctive melody or sound, usually at the start or end of a commercial.
2. It should be present every time the brand is heard.
3. Intel logo (Walter Werzowa, 1994): three seconds, four notes matching the syllables of "Intel Inside", intervals of a fourth and a fifth to avoid cultural association, plus one opening "palate cleanser" note.
4. His claim: a mnemonic longer than three seconds shifts people "from hearing to listening" and works more consciously.
5. Start from the client's problem, not from music.
6. Do not run a five-company contest; judging about 150 two-second logos is numbing.
7. Guest's figure: "40% of the time" TV viewers are on a second screen or not looking, so audio must carry the brand.
8. Using a familiar real-world sound (Southwest's seat-belt chime) makes people recall the brand elsewhere.

## PART B - written sources (all opened)

B1. IPA / Binet and Field, "The Long and the Short of It" - Thinkbox summary, 6 Dec 2012 - https://www.thinkbox.tv/research/thinkbox-research/the-long-and-the-short-of-it/ (COMMUNITY summary of an OFFICIAL IPA report)
- About 1,000 campaigns, 30 years of IPA data, more than 700 brands, over 80 categories.
- "At least 60%" of budget into brand-building media.
- Whole-market targeting gives "3 times as many large business effects" as targeting existing customers.
- Campaigns targeting new customers: "60% more large sales effects in the first six months".
- Long-term (3+ years) investment delivers "double the profit" of short-term (under 1 year); both together is higher.
- Second summary (Alex Murrell, 22 Sep 2021, https://www.alexmurrell.co.uk/summaries/les-binet-and-peter-field-the-long-and-the-short-of-it): 996 campaigns 1980-2010; balance "around 60:40"; emotional campaigns "almost twice as likely" to give top-box profit growth; fame campaigns report the greatest profit growth.
- The IPA's own slide deck lists 10 principles including "Balance head and heart", "Aim for fame", "Creativity increases efficiency" (https://ipa.co.uk/media/14463/long_and_short_of_it_presentation_final_030424.pdf).

B2. System1 / Orlando Wood - https://system1group.com/blog/the-timeless-importance-of-the-show (2 Nov 2023) and https://system1group.com/blog/lemon-preview (3 Oct 2019). VENDOR.
- Right-brain features "character, narrative, place and depth" consistently drive greater effectiveness.
- 150 Amazon Freevee ads: left-brain ads were "twice as likely" to drive a detail-page view; right-brain elements gave "a 1.6x uplift" in the brand being remembered.
- Left-brain style = "rhythmic rapid cuts, obtrusive text, and disconnected scenes".
- "Moto e azione": find the story's turning point and show it on faces and bodies.
- Fluent Device = recurring character. Database behind Lemon: "more than 30,000 ads".

B3. Kantar + WARC, "The art of proof" - https://www.kantar.com/north-america/Inspiration/Advertising-Media/The-art-of-proof-How-creative-quality-drives-profit (date not shown). VENDOR.
- About 450 ads matched to WARC profit data; the most creative and effective ads generate "more than four times as much profit".
- The long-term measure related to profit more strongly than short-term persuasion.
- Strong ads "show the product continuously throughout", use "well-integrated product demonstrations" and make "the brand appear early".
- Branding devices: slogans, celebrities, music and sonic branding.

B4. Nielsen / NCSolutions - https://www.nielsen.com/insights/2017/when-it-comes-to-advertising-effectiveness-what-is-key/ (9 Oct 2017). VENDOR.
- Creative is still the largest sales driver; in 2006 it was 65% of sales lift.
- Media's share rose "to 36% from 15%" over 11 years. Nearly 500 FMCG campaigns.
- Weak creative gives weak lift. For weekend-bought products, Thursday/Friday ads gave "almost three times" more lift.

B5. Ipsos, "The Power of You" - https://www.ipsos.com/en/power-you-why-distinctive-brand-assets-are-driving-force-creative-effectiveness (Feb 2020). VENDOR.
- Over 2,000 video ads analysed.
- Brand assets link to branded attention more than just showing or naming the brand.
- Own assets (characters, sonic cues) beat borrowed ones (celebrities, music); audio assets are used less but are on average more effective than some visual ones.
- Ehrenberg-Bass's own distinctive-asset pages gave no readable content (sponsor-only); fame and uniqueness grid is SNIPPET ONLY.

B6. StudioBinder shot sizes (24 Feb 2025, updated 5 Mar 2026) https://www.studiobinder.com/blog/types-of-camera-shots-sizes-in-film/ and angles (7 Jan 2025) https://www.studiobinder.com/blog/types-of-camera-shot-angles-in-film/. VENDOR.
- Sizes: extreme wide, wide, full (head to toe), medium long (knees up), cowboy (mid-thigh up), medium (waist up), medium close-up (chest up), close-up, extreme close-up (often a macro lens).
- "Shot size contrast" is what signals importance.
- Shoulder-level is more standard than eye-level; overhead is about 90 degrees; Dutch angle for tension.

B7. PremiumBeat product shots - https://www.premiumbeat.com/blog/lighting-and-shooting-product-shots/ (5 Jun 2019) and https://www.premiumbeat.com/blog/complete-guide-shooting-product-shots/ (27 Jun 2018). COMMUNITY.
- Orbit the product to create depth; "you almost always see this in TV commercials".
- Order: hard backlight for edge, points of light in the background, then a long tube light "45 degrees above and 45 degrees in front".
- Translucent products: light from below; glass and liquid: "light for the reflections" (acrylic sheet).
- Non-reflective objects: diffused top light. Small-product slider radius two feet, with a macro lens.

B8. Google Veo prompt guide - https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini-enterprise-agent-platform/models/video/video-gen-prompt-guide ("Last updated 2026-11-20 UTC"). OFFICIAL.
- Prompt parts: subject, action, scene, camera angle, camera movement, lens and optical effects, lighting, tone, artistic style, ambiance, temporal elements, audio.
- Movements named: static, pan, tilt, dolly, truck, pedestal, zoom, crane, aerial, handheld, arc.
- Lens terms: wide-angle, telephoto, shallow depth of field, lens flare, rack focus, fisheye, dolly zoom.
- Lighting terms: Rembrandt, high-key, low-key, volumetric, backlighting, golden hour.
- Warning on the page: some advanced camera angles and lenses "are not officially supported".

B9. Storyboard and animatic - StudioBinder https://www.studiobinder.com/blog/how-to-make-a-storyboard/ (16 Mar 2025, updated 11 Nov 2026) and Boords https://boords.com/animatic/what-is-the-definition-of-an-animatic-storyboard (date unclear). VENDOR.
- Storyboard steps: choose layout (12 small panels a page, or large frames for pitching), understand the scene, make or gather images, add arrows, add notes, share for comments.
- Animatic = storyboard as video; each frame held as long as its shot, against scratch voiceover, music or effects; made before design or filming.
- "An animatic is the first time your team and client see the project in motion."

B10. Local business ads - MNTN Research https://research.mountain.com/creative-analysis/crazier-the-better-the-art-and-science-of-local-tv-ads (5 Jan 2023, updated 16 Apr 2026) and Comcast Advertising https://comcastadvertising.com/insights/knowledge-hub/local-tv-advertising/ (5 Jan 2026). VENDOR.
- Of memorable local ads submitted in MNTN's survey, "60% prominently featured contact information" and "40% included a catch phrase or tagline"; 24% were car dealerships.
- Three common elements: where to go, what to do (visual plus spoken call to action), an earworm.
- Keep the address or URL on screen throughout; recurring characters help recognition.
- "You don't need a big production budget if your idea is strong."
- Comcast: "simple messaging and a strong call to action", tailored to the community.

## CONFLICTS BETWEEN EXPERTS
1. Polish: Hott says ads that look like ads are skipped and AI can be "too perfect"; Curious Refuge adds 4K upscale, letterbox and grain for a cinematic finish; Kantar and System1 tie profit to creative quality and "putting on a show". Different arenas: Hott speaks about paid social feeds, the others about brand films and TV.
2. Product presence: Kantar says show the product throughout and brand early; the Cadillac example System1 praises never showed the product; file 13's Flick video hid the product until the end.
3. Sell now or build memory: System1's Amazon data gives left-brain ads double the immediate page views and right-brain ads 1.6x the memory; Binet and Field want about 60% on brand; MNTN treats local advertisers as performance marketers who need contact details and a call to action.
4. Targeting: Binet and Field say talk to the whole market; Denney and Hott build on personas and hyper-relevance.
5. Treatment time: IPA/APA say at least a week; Mark Bone says he gets a couple of days, sometimes one.
6. Animation model: Curious Refuge prefers Kling for quality and Flow/Veo for cost; Dan Kieft (file 13) uses Seedance 2.5 in Higgsfield. No source read compares them on the same shot.
7. Frame rate: Curious Refuge says 24 online and 30 broadcast; file 07's UK Clearcast spec is 25.
8. Angle default: StudioBinder calls shoulder level more standard than eye level; In Depth Cine says most shots are at eye level.
9. Sound logo: Werzowa says three seconds and constructed, not composed; MNTN's local ads rely on longer jingles and catchphrases.
