### 1E. AI-specific contract clauses (AICP guidelines dated 10.21.26)
Source for all rows: https://aicp.nyc3.digitaloceanspaces.com/content/AI-Guidelines-for-production-and-post-production-contracts_FINAL.10.21.26.pdf - OFFICIAL (trade body; it states the guidelines are optional per company).
| Point | Wording |
|---|---|
| Define AI use | Use, purpose, caveats and any prohibited uses "should be set out in the agreement" |
| Platform criteria | Look for one that (i) gives enterprise or commercial terms that indemnify for IP infringement; (ii) "does not train on inputs or outputs"; (iii) permits advertiser use without restriction; (iv) does not use output for other purposes. If the required platform fails these, get written acknowledgement |
| Ownership | "The producer should retain ownership of its inputs/prompts, while the advertiser will ... typically seek to retain ownership of the final output"; prompts treated as confidential intermediate materials |
| No copyright warranty | Sample clause: "agency and advertiser acknowledge and agree that [materials] created by generative AI may not, in whole or in part, be subject to copyright or other intellectual property protections" |
| Producer warranties | No inputs likely to give infringing outputs; "commercially reasonable human review"; but "agency/advertiser shall be responsible for final clearance"; no client confidential data into AI tools without permission |
| Indemnity | Limited to breach of chosen warranties and "to the extent of the indemnification provision provided by the applicable AI platform, if any"; check whether platform indemnity extends to the advertiser |
| Client-side risk | Advertiser indemnifies for its own inputs, for "disclosure requirements, including the disclosure of AI generated humans or synthetic performers", for talent likeness use, and for platforms it designated |
| No training on deliverables | Client "should be prohibited from using/reverse engineering the producer's deliverables to train AI platforms" |
| Records | Producer should "archive prompts/inputs in their entirety" |
| Talent | Digital-replica release language should come from agency/advertiser, scoped "for this project only and not in perpetuity" |

US Copyright Office, Part 2 (Issue No. 1060, January 29, 2025) - OFFICIAL - https://www.copyright.gov/newsnet/2025/1060.html: outputs "can be protected by copyright only where a human author has determined sufficient expressive elements"; this includes a human-authored work perceptible in the output or "creative arrangements or modifications of the output, but not the mere provision of prompts"; AI assistance "does not bar copyrightability".
INFERENCE (from the Office text plus AICP clause 6): a pure-prompt output may leave nothing copyrightable to assign in the US, so a contract can only assign "whatever rights exist"; protection attaches to human-made script, edit, selection/arrangement, music and design. Not legal advice; no Israeli ruling on this was found.
